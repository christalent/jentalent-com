About this electronic journal

This electronic journal is kept in addition to your tangible journal — it does not replace it. Under WAC 308-30-200(2)(a), an electronic notarial journal may only be maintained in addition to the tangible notarial journal required by RCW 42.45.180. Your tangible journal remains the primary record of your notarial acts.

Each entry here records what the law requires: the date and time of the notarial act, a description of the record and the type of notarial act, the full name and address of the person for whom the act was performed, how you identified that person, the person's signature, and the number of statements or documents notarized.

This journal is protected by a password, entries are linked in a tamper-evident hash chain you can check at any time, and you can download a backup and produce a copy of any entry. Keep your tangible journal up to date, and keep the journal for 10 years after the last notarial act chronicled in it (RCW 42.45.180(1)). The journal is your exclusive property and is not surrendered to an employer (WAC 308-30-200(3)).

This page describes what the tool does. It is not legal advice — if you have questions about Washington notarial journal requirements, contact the Washington Secretary of State.

Reset the password

If you have forgotten the password, you can reset it here. Resetting the password does not delete your journal entries — they stay exactly where they are on this device and will be shown again as soon as you set a new password.

The password is stored only as a salted hash on this device. This lock keeps the journal private on a shared computer; it is not a substitute for keeping your tangible journal secure.
